Cause-Vetting Tool

Pressure-test the cause you want to carry all semester. This tool does not decide for you, and it does not read your writing. It hands you concrete tests, you check the ones that are truly true, and it shows you where each bar lands.
This runs entirely on your computer. Nothing is sent anywhere. You'll name a candidate cause, then for each of six bars you'll answer a few yes-or-no tests you can actually verify (can you point to it, could you photograph it, would a stranger get it). The tool adds up your checks into a Strong, Shaky, or Not-really reading for that bar, then applies one transparent rule to the whole set. You make the final call.
How each bar is scored (no mystery, and no guessing your own vibe):
  • Each bar has three concrete tests. All three true = Strong. Two = Shaky. One or none = Not really.
  • Three bars are non-negotiable: you care, real & specific, and safe & ethical. A "Not really" on any of those = rework or swap.
  • A "Not really" on low-traction, media job, or lasts-a-semester, or three-plus "Shaky" overall = reconsider / reshape.
  • One or two "Shaky" and no fatal problems = go, with a tune-up. All six "Strong" = go.
Specific beats vast, every time. "Campus safety" is too big to grip. "A crosswalk at that one deadly corner" you can actually move. Write the smallest true version.

Test it against the six bars

Your cause

The real, low-traction cause you pick in Week 1 and carry through the whole course: the weekly Field Notebook, and all three projects. Its obscurity is the point.

Example: not "hunger" but "the campus food pantry runs out of protein every Thursday and nobody knows it's there." Small enough that your semester of media work can genuinely change it.

Real & specific

A cause you can point at and name, with edges. Big abstractions can't be gripped or moved in a semester; specific things can.

With vs. without: "the environment" (no edges, no handle) becomes "the storm drain on Faculty St dumps straight into the creek, unmarked." Now there's something to photograph, explain, and fix.

Low-traction

Under-covered, under-organized, not already viral. The opposite of a cause with millions of people and dollars behind it.

Why it matters here: on a huge cause your work vanishes into the crowd. On a quiet local one, a single well-made campaign can be the thing that tips it. Low-traction is where your effort is most meaningful and most measurable.

A media job to do

Real communication work that isn't done yet: people who need reaching, a story nobody's told well, or a scattered group who've never been gathered into a shared world.

Example: "The affected tenants have never met each other" is a media job (gather them). "Everyone already knows and agrees" is not, there's nothing left to communicate.

Safe & ethical

Your media work should not endanger, expose, or exploit anyone, including you. If real people are at the center, especially vulnerable ones, you need their genuine consent before you put them online.

Example: a cause about unhoused neighbors can be powerful and ethical if they're partners who agreed, or harmful if you photograph and post people who never said yes. Same cause, opposite ethics.
