Schedule

Sixteen weeks, grouped into five units. Each week has a Tuesday analog case in class and a Thursday digital module. Read left to right, then down. Click any week to open its module. Exact calendar dates are posted in Canvas.

Key deadlines

  1. Week 1: your cause is chosen and locked
  2. Week 3: Project 1 introduced
  3. Week 6: Project 1 (Movement Media Analysis) due
  4. Week 8: Field Notebook midterm check-in
  5. Week 9: Project 2 set up
  6. Week 11: Project 2 (Content Strategy & Audit) due
  7. Week 12: Project 3 set up
  8. Weeks 15–16: campaign presentations
  9. Week 16: Project 3 (Campaign + Impact Report) due and Field Notebook end-of-term
Unit A · Foundations: what "social" media actually is Weeks 1–3
Week 1 both on campus
Social Media Is Older Than Your Phone
TueCommunication as ritual vs. transmission (Carey); your own media and attention audit; begin the cause hunt.
ThuDeepen Carey, set up the Field Notebook, run the cause-vetting tool. Lock your cause.
Week 2 both on campus
The Medium Is Not Neutral
Tue"The medium is the message" (McLuhan) and "Do artifacts have politics?" (Winner); slow vs. fast media; make and circulate something by hand.
ThuDiagnose the medium: trade-offs of fast, mixed, and slow media, applied to your cause.
Week 3
The Federalist Papers
TuePublics, pseudonymity ("Publius"), and persuading a country of strangers in 1788.
ThuHow ideas circulate on platforms now, plus a rhetorical-analysis toolkit. Project 1 introduced.
Unit B · Movements & their media theories Weeks 4–8 · toward Project 1
Week 4
The Abolitionist Press
TueFrederick Douglass and The North Star: owning your own medium, moral suasion, the paper as movement infrastructure.
ThuAnalyze a movement's media strategy; ethics intro (disclosure, representation).
Week 5
Suffragist Spectacle & Visual Rhetoric
TueThe 1913 procession, banners, sashes, color-coding; arguing with image and design (Triggs).
ThuComposing meaning through image and design; P1 workshop.
Week 6
Samizdat
TueUnderground self-publishing under censorship; slow media, trust networks, real risk; "the reader is the publisher."
ThuPrivacy, surveillance, and disclosure ethics.
Project 1 (Movement Media Analysis) due
Week 7
The Black Panther Party Newspaper
TueCommunity media and self-representation; Emory Douglas' art; distribution as organizing.
ThuRepresentation ethics and accessibility.
Week 8 Fall Break
ACT UP
TueFall break, no class. Design activism (SILENCE=DEATH, Gran Fury), direct action engineered for attention, affinity groups.
ThuAccessible, inclusive multimodal design. Module is optional extra credit this week.
Field Notebook midterm check-in due
Unit C · Strategy: building connection on purpose Weeks 9–11 · toward Project 2
Week 9
The Zapatistas
TueCommuniqués from Chiapas; slow (indigenous assembly) and fast (early internet, global solidarity) working together.
ThuContent strategy and the audit. Project 2 set up.
Week 10
Networked Protest & Its Limits
TueScaling without organizational muscle and the "tactical freeze" (Tufekci).
ThuAudience, goals, editorial calendar, and platform-appropriate voice.
Week 11
Communicative Capitalism & Attention
TueWhen posting feels like politics but changes nothing (Jodi Dean); refusing the attention economy (Jenny Odell).
ThuFinalize your strategy.
Project 2 (Content Strategy & Audit) due
Unit D · The campaign: put the social back Weeks 12–14 · toward Project 3
Week 12
Relational Organizing & the Ground Game
TueField research shows face-to-face contact beats broadcast; the most powerful medium is a trusted person.
ThuDesign an ethical, multimodal campaign. Project 3 set up.
Week 13
Emergent Strategy
Tueadrienne maree brown: small is all, fractal, "move at the speed of trust," critical connections over critical mass.
ThuProduce multimodal texts: language, image, sound, and design.
Week 14
Measuring Circulation & Impact
TueAnalytics and engagement vs. vanity metrics: what actually counts as impact for a cause.
ThuBuild a simple, honest impact framework. P3 built and the impact report drafted.
Unit E · Presentations & close Weeks 15–16
Week 15 Thanksgiving
Campaign Presentations
TuePresentations, Day 1: each student gives a 5-minute talk on their P3 campaign and impact report.
ThuThanksgiving break. No class, no module, nothing due.
Week 16
Presentations & Course Close
TuePresentations, Day 2 (the rest of the class), then the course close: where we go from here, keeping your cause organized, and how these skills transfer.
ThuNo class. Tuesday is the semester's last meeting.
Project 3 (Campaign + Impact Report) due · Field Notebook end-of-term
