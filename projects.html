Major Projects

Three projects carry the course, and each one builds on the last. You analyze how a movement works, plan a strategy for your own cause, then make and measure a real campaign. Each project is scored on a handful of criteria, and the weekly modules walk you through building it.

How to read a rubric. Each criterion is scored on one of four levels. Meets is the target: solid, full-credit work. Read the Meets column first, that's what you're aiming for on every line.
Exceeds goes beyond the target Meets the target (your goal) Developing partway there Not yet not present yet
Project 1 · introduced Week 3 · due Week 620%

Movement Media Analysis

Research-grounded analysis · submitted as a Google Doc

Analyze how one movement, one of our cases or one you bring, built connection and spread ideas through its media. Name its "media theory," read its slow and fast tools, and weigh its ethics. Then pull concrete lessons for your own cause. This is close reading of how a movement actually reached people, backed by real sources and primary artifacts.

The rubric

Media theory, named and applied
ExceedsNames the movement's implicit media theory with precision and uses it to explain choices others would miss
MeetsIdentifies the movement's media theory (ritual/transmission, slow/fast, owning vs. renting the channel) and uses it to drive the analysis
DevelopingGestures at a theory but mostly describes what happened
Not yetNo guiding idea; a summary of events
Evidence & primary artifacts
ExceedsWell-chosen artifacts and sources, closely read and fully cited
MeetsGrounded in real artifacts and credible sources, cited consistently
DevelopingSome evidence, thin or loosely cited
Not yetLittle evidence, or uncited
Analysis of connection & circulation
ExceedsShows exactly how the media built community and moved ideas, including tensions and trade-offs
MeetsExplains how the media drew people together and circulated the message, not merely that it did
DevelopingNotices effects but doesn't explain the mechanism
Not yetDescribes the movement without analyzing its media
Lessons for your cause
ExceedsSharp, specific, defensible lessons that reshape how you'd work your cause
MeetsDraws concrete, usable lessons for your own cause from the case
DevelopingGeneric takeaways that could apply to anything
Not yetNo connection to your cause

Content Strategy & Audit

Strategy document · submitted as a Google Doc

For your cause, take an honest read of where it stands right now, then build a strategy to move it forward. Audit the current presence, define the audience and real goals, choose channels and a platform-appropriate voice, and lay out a workable editorial calendar. The test is whether someone could pick this up and run the cause's communications from it.

The rubric

Audit
ExceedsClear-eyed audit that separates real traction from vanity and finds the gaps others miss
MeetsReads the cause's current channels, audience, and what is and isn't working, with candor
DevelopingDescribes the presence but doesn't evaluate it
Not yetNo real audit
Audience & goals
ExceedsSpecific audiences and goals that are measurable and genuinely reachable
MeetsDefines real audience(s) and concrete goals tied to the cause's needs
DevelopingVague audience ("everyone") or fuzzy goals
Not yetNo defined audience or goals
Strategy & platform-appropriate voice
ExceedsChannel choices are justified and the voice is tuned convincingly to each platform and audience
MeetsChannel choices fit the goals; voice suits both the platform and the cause
DevelopingSome choices unexplained, or one voice pasted everywhere
Not yetNo coherent strategy or voice
Editorial calendar & feasibility
ExceedsA concrete, sustainable calendar someone could actually run, with contingencies
MeetsA clear editorial calendar sized to real capacity
DevelopingA rough plan, over-ambitious or thin
Not yetNo calendar or plan

Campaign + Impact Report

Multimodal campaign + written report · presented to the class

Design, produce, and present a small, ethical, multimodal campaign for your cause, grounded in one of the media theories from the course, then measure its circulation and impact. While traditional social media channels will likely be part of your campaign, they should not be the focus of it, and you do not have to include them at all if you can justify the effectiveness of analog methods. Then measure and reflect: what circulated, what actually changed, and what counts as real impact versus noise. You present the campaign and its results in the final weeks (the presentation itself is scored under Participation & Final Presentation).

The rubric

Multimodal campaign (craft & coherence)
ExceedsPolished pieces across modes that clearly belong to one campaign and reinforce each other
MeetsReal artifacts combining language, image, sound, or design into a coherent campaign
DevelopingA few pieces, loosely related or uneven in craft
Not yetOne mode only, or no finished artifacts
Grounded in a course media theory
ExceedsA named theory visibly shapes every design choice; the campaign is an argument for that approach
MeetsA named course theory clearly informs the campaign's design and channel choices
DevelopingA theory is mentioned but doesn't drive the work
Not yetNo theoretical grounding
Ethics: disclosure, representation, accessibility
ExceedsEthics are designed in from the start; accessible, fair, transparent throughout
MeetsDiscloses with candor, represents people fairly, and meets basic accessibility (alt text, captions, contrast)
DevelopingSome ethical gaps or accessibility oversights
Not yetMisleading, unfair, or inaccessible
Impact report (honest metrics)
ExceedsDefines real success metrics, measures them, and reflects with unusual honesty on what changed and what didn't
MeetsNames real impact metrics (behavior, relationships, reach) over vanity ones, and reflects on the results
DevelopingReports mostly likes and follows, with little reflection
Not yetNo measurement or reflection

Field Notebook

A private Google Sheet · turned in twice

Your working notebook holds the in-class exercises, your analog experiments (a real undistracted conversation, a note circulated by hand, a day off the feed, a face-to-face ask), case notes, platform observations, and strategy scratch. It's graded on completion, not polish: full credit for doing the thinking and the fieldwork. You turn it in at the midterm check-in and again at the end of term.

The rubric

Weekly entries present
ExceedsEvery week complete, with extra digging where it got interesting
MeetsAn entry for each week's prompt, done on time
DevelopingSeveral weeks missing or one-line
Not yetMostly empty
Analog experiments done
ExceedsExperiments actually attempted in the world and documented with real observation
MeetsThe analog experiments are done and briefly written up
DevelopingA few skipped or described without doing them
Not yetNot attempted
Applied to your cause
ExceedsEach week visibly moves your cause thinking forward
MeetsEntries connect the week's idea to your own cause
DevelopingGeneric notes that skip the cause
Not yetNo link to your cause
Documentation & observation
ExceedsSpecific, noticing detail; screenshots, quotes, and notes that could seed a project
MeetsConcrete observations captured, not bare opinions
DevelopingVague or purely reactive notes
Not yetNo usable documentation

Participation & Final Presentation

In-class engagement + a campaign presentation

Ten percent covers showing up ready and joining the class conversation all semester, plus your final presentation of the P3 campaign and impact report. A strong presentation tells the cause's story, shows the actual work, and reports the results with candor, including what didn't land.

The rubric

Participation
ExceedsA generous, prepared presence that lifts the whole room's thinking
MeetsComes ready, engages the exercises, and contributes to the class conversation
DevelopingPresent but often passive or unprepared
Not yetFrequently absent or disengaged
Presentation: story & the work
ExceedsTells the cause's story compellingly and shows the campaign so the room can see the choices
MeetsClearly presents the cause, the campaign, and the thinking behind it
DevelopingShows the work but the story or logic is unclear
Not yetDisorganized or incomplete
Presentation: honest results
ExceedsReports real impact candidly, names what didn't work, and draws a lesson from it
MeetsReports the campaign's results with candor, including limits
DevelopingReports only the flattering numbers
Not yetNo results, or misleading ones
