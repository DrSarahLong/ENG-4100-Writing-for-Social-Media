The skills you are leaving with

These do not stay in this classroom. They travel to any job, any platform, any cause you take up for the rest of your life.

Read a medium

You can look at any channel and ask what it makes easy, what it buries, and who it quietly leaves out. That is rhetorical analysis, and every employer wants it.

Slow and fast, on purpose

You know reach is not trust, and that lasting things get built slowly. You can pick the right speed for the job instead of defaulting to the loudest one.

Tell a true story

You can make a stranger care about something obscure in ten seconds, and back it with a claim that holds up. That is the whole game in marketing, nonprofits, and organizing.

Measure what matters

You can tell a vanity metric from a real result and say so plainly. In a world that runs on dashboards, honest measurement is rare and valuable.

Build with people, not at them

From relational organizing to emergent strategy, you learned that the strongest medium is a trusted person. That works in a team, a community, and a movement.

Compose across modes

Marshall McLuhan

The Week 2 theorist behind "the medium is the message." His claim: a medium's biggest effect is not the content it carries but how it reshapes the way we perceive and relate. You now read every platform this way.

Langdon Winner

"Do artifacts have politics?" His answer: yes. Design can enforce power quietly, no law required. The low bridge that keeps buses out, the bench you cannot lie on, the algorithm that decides who sees you.

Putting the "social" back

The frame of the whole course: real connection and community-building is a skill, and much of it got lost as "social" media optimized for reach over relationship. Every case you studied was a lesson in building the trust a feed cannot.

Rhetorical analysis

Reading a message, or a medium, for how it works on an audience: what it assumes, what it rewards, who it addresses, who it excludes.

Example: looking at a recruiting flyer and naming its appeal, its target, and the person it quietly writes off. You did this to platforms all semester.

Relational organizing

From Week 12: a real conversation with someone who knows you moves people that ads and mailers cannot. The most powerful medium is a trusted person.

Example: one neighbor asking another to show up beats any boosted post. This works far past this course, in any team or community.

adrienne maree brown · emergent strategy

From Week 13: "small is good, small is all"; move at the speed of trust; critical connections over critical mass. Movements grow like mycelium, not like ad campaigns.

Example: instead of chasing a viral moment, you deepen a handful of real relationships and let them spread. That is the next-step advice for your cause.
